Voice
Plain, warm and unhurried. The app is doing something quietly extraordinary and says so without raising its voice.
The rule that governs the rest
Say what a thing is, does, and will do.
Defining something by what it is not leaves everything else standing as an open question, and a reader has to infer the shape from the hole. A negative also reads as a warning even when it was meant as a reassurance.
It stays on this phone.
It never leaves the phone.
Drawn once, and yours.
It cannot be drawn again.
A brand new and unique seed will be formed every time.
No two seeds are ever the same.
Your plant can meet others if you tap their device.
Plants will not grow unless you meet someone.
Short, and set large
Sixteen words at 16 pt reach a reader that twenty-seven at 14 pt do not. Cut until each line carries one idea.
Second person, present tense
Your plant, this moment, this phone. The app talks about what is happening now rather than what the software supports.
Explain a function where it is met
Each thing is described at the point someone first arrives at it, and retires once they have used it.
Concrete over abstract
The coordinates of this moment. Tap their device. Weeks. Names for things a person can picture.
British English
Colour, neighbour, realise. Sentence case in body copy; the headings are uppercased by the type style rather than by the writing.
Quiet punctuation
Full stops and the occasional colon. The app has no exclamation marks in it and wants none.
The passages shown when two plants make a seed follow the same rule: they hold something positive for the meeting, and each carries its source in brief beneath it.
